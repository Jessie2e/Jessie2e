# How to Get Your Small Business Found by ChatGPT and AI Search in 2026

By Jessie Towey, 2e Studio | Updated October 27, 2026

When someone asks an AI tool for a dog trainer, accountant, landscaper, or website designer, you want your business to be a sensible option. The practical starting point is to make your business easy to understand, give people evidence they can check, and make your website accessible to search systems. Then measure whether that visibility leads to real inquiries and paying customers.

There is no setting that guarantees a ChatGPT recommendation. There is also no need to buy a collection of secret prompts before you can begin.

I run 2e Studio, an independent web design business based near Smith Lake, Alabama. In October 2026, we tested our own visibility in Google and ChatGPT, checked how our website was being accessed, and published a small set of technical improvements. This guide combines that experience with official documentation. It also explains what we have **not** established: we do not yet have evidence that those changes produced new customers.

Allow **30–60 minutes for an initial review**: check one public page, write down your offer, save three discovery questions, and note what needs help. Completing the website checks, one page improvement, and tracking setup may take **about 95–150 minutes in total**, spread over a few days. Account setup, developer work, provider processing, and indexing can take longer. Plan about 15 minutes a week for records, then review after 30 days.

## Start with the result you actually want

“AI visibility” can describe several different things:

| What happened | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| An answer names your business | Your name appeared; check that it means your business |
| An answer links to your website | Your site was cited; open the link and check the destination |
| An answer suggests hiring you | You were presented as a possible provider in that answer |
| Someone visits your website | A visit happened; its source or intent may still be unknown |
| Someone sends a relevant inquiry | A potential customer took a meaningful next step |
| Someone pays you | You received money; record the amount and how the person found you |

These are separate observations. A citation is not a sale, and a direct website visit does not prove an AI referral. Your useful business question is: **Are previously unaware customers finding us, contacting us, and buying?**

## 1. Test the questions a new customer would ask

Start with three realistic questions. Leave out your business name so you are testing discovery rather than recognition.

- Service and place: “Who provides bookkeeping for small businesses in Huntsville, Alabama?”
- Budget: “I need a small-business website and have about $500. What are realistic options?”
- Constraint: “Who can help maintain a small website without requiring a long contract?”

Replace these with your actual service, location, budget, and buying concerns. Do not use a budget you cannot serve just because it produces a favorable answer.

Use a fresh conversation. Turn personalization off when the tool offers that option, and choose a web-search mode when you want to test current web discovery. Write down the platform, mode, date, location context, exact question, and what happened. Account history and location can still influence results, so this is a practical sample, not a neutral census of the internet.

If your business appears, open its cited link. Does the destination belong to you? Is the location right? Are the services and prices accurate? Save the answer or a short evidence note. A failed response belongs in your log as “unavailable,” not “we did not appear.”

Ask a separate branded question, such as “What does [business name] in [town] do?” to check factual accuracy. Keep that result separate from your discovery tests. Someone who already knows your name is a different audience from a new buyer.

### What happened in our own test

On October 27, 2026, we completed seven Google AI Mode questions, one Google AI Overview example, and one successful ChatGPT web-search answer. None of those nine usable answers correctly named 2e Studio or cited jessie2e.com. Two additional ChatGPT attempts failed to load and were excluded. Copilot was unavailable and was not counted as a negative result.

One personalized answer did name “2e Studio,” but connected the name to an unrelated Philadelphia studio’s Instagram account. Here is the useful distinction:

- **What looked promising:** our business name appeared in an answer.
- **What the link showed:** a different business, in a different place.
- **How we recorded it:** an incorrect identity match, excluded from our discovery results.

The lesson is to check identity, not just celebrate a mention. Nine answers are also too few to establish overall market visibility or predict future recommendations.

## 2. Make sure your public pages can be reached

Open your homepage and main service page in a private browser window. Can a visitor read them without logging in, entering a password, or dismissing a security challenge? Can they find the contact action?

Next, ask the person who manages your website to check three things: the intended public pages can be crawled, they do not carry an accidental `noindex` instruction, and their main content is available to search systems. “Crawled” means a system can fetch a page. “Indexed” means it has been included in a search engine’s collection. Neither guarantees a recommendation.

Google says pages supporting its AI Overviews and AI Mode must be indexed and eligible to appear with a search snippet. It does not require a special AI schema or an extra AI file. Start with ordinary search eligibility. [Google’s AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

Use Google Search Console’s URL Inspection for your homepage and one important service page. Review the actual reason if a page is excluded. A redirect or an alternative URL can be intentional. Submitting a sitemap helps explain your site’s pages; it does not order a search engine to index them.

For nontechnical owners, a useful request is: “Please check whether these two public pages are accessible, indexable, and showing the right canonical URL. Tell me which issue you actually observed before changing anything.” A canonical URL is the preferred address for a page when more than one address can reach it.

## 3. Keep search access and training choices separate

AI companies can use different systems to find current web pages and to collect material for model training. Allowing one does not necessarily require allowing the other. OpenAI, for example, documents separate controls for ChatGPT search and model training. [OpenAI's explanation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)

You do not need to edit these controls yourself. If an access problem is suspected, ask your website person: **“Can the search system reach our public pages while keeping our existing training preferences and security protections?”** Ask them to identify an actual blocking rule before changing it.

Your site's crawler instructions and your hosting security settings are separate places to check. A successful visit in your browser does not establish access for every provider. Avoid blanket “allow everything” changes. In our own checks, we preserved training restrictions and did not establish that crawler blocking explained our missing recommendations.

## 4. Explain the business in language a buyer can use

A page saying “We create exceptional solutions” makes a visitor do too much guessing. Explain the actual service and the practical boundaries.

On your main service page, make these answers easy to find:

- What do you do, and who is it for?
- Where do you work? Distinguish your actual location from places you serve.
- What does the service include? What needs a separate quote?
- What does it cost, or what determines the price?
- Are there ongoing fees, commitments, or maintenance responsibilities?
- What should someone do next?

A fictional example, for a bookkeeping business that really offers these terms:

**Vague:** “Reliable bookkeeping solutions for growing businesses.”

**Useful:** “Monthly bookkeeping for owner-operated service businesses in Huntsville and Madison. Plans start at $250 per month for up to 100 transactions. Payroll and tax preparation are separate. Request a consultation to confirm your scope.”

The example is a writing pattern, not a recommended price. Replace every detail with your real offer. If you do not publish prices, explain the factors that affect a quote and what the first conversation involves.

You do not need to redesign your homepage or add a large FAQ section to do this. Improve unclear sentences or use an existing service page. Google’s broader guidance emphasizes useful, distinctive information and accessible content; it does not offer a guaranteed recommendation formula. [Google’s optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

For 2e Studio, we kept the existing offer, pricing, and homepage design. Our October 27 changes focused on technical search basics, Bing verification, and making it easier for buyers to mention an AI tool in our existing inquiry-source question. We have not yet measured whether these changes brought new customers.

## 5. Give people evidence they can examine

Choose one real project, customer problem, or example that helps a buyer judge your work. Describe the situation, what you delivered, and where someone can see it. If you have a measured result, give the date, period, and method. If you do not, explain the work without inventing a performance claim.

For a landscaping business, that might be before-and-after photographs with permission, the actual scope, and the town served. For a designer, it might be a live website and a short account of the client’s needs. A concept project can demonstrate skill, but label it as a concept.

Check important third-party profiles you already use. Your name, service, location, and website link should agree with your own site. Correct an old address or broken link before opening ten new accounts. Ask clients for honest feedback through the review channels appropriate to your business; do not write reviews for them or invent testimonials.

These actions help people verify you. Our experiment does not establish that a particular review count, directory, or wording causes an AI system to recommend a business.

## 6. Keep the technical work focused

You do not need a rebuild because AI search exists. Ask your developer to confirm the essentials:

1. Important public pages load successfully and have readable main content.
2. Navigation links reach those pages directly at their preferred addresses.
3. Titles and descriptions accurately describe each page.
4. Structured data, if present, agrees with visible facts and does not duplicate existing business entities.
5. Old addresses redirect appropriately, and genuinely missing pages return an actual 404.
6. Contact and booking controls work on a phone.

Structured data is machine-readable information about a page or business. It can help describe facts; it does not certify quality or buy a recommendation. Our site already had Organization, Person, WebSite, and Service information. We kept it rather than adding duplicate “AI schema.”

Be cautious about packages built around mandatory `llms.txt` files, hundreds of near-identical location pages, invented reviews, or a promised number of AI placements. Ask which provider requires the proposed change and what evidence would show it worked. A vendor’s claim is not that evidence.

## 7. Connect free reports and read their limits

Verify your site in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools using the accounts and domain you control. Use your platform’s existing verification tools or ask your developer for help. Keep verification tags in place after approval. Submit the sitemap your site actually uses.

In our jessie2e.com Search Console property, the **Generative AI features** report, marked **Beta**, showed two impressions for September 28–October 25, 2026: one for the homepage and one for our Sarah Pickett project page. We rechecked that view and preserved a dated screenshot and its displayed text on October 27. The view showed impressions without a separate click metric. This is an observation from our account, not a promise that every account has the same report. Check the labels and dates in yours; reporting interfaces change.

Our general Google report showed 34 impressions and eight clicks. I frequently search for the business, so some or all of that activity may have been ours. We cannot call those eight clicks eight prospects. External customer acquisition was unknown, potentially zero.

Bing’s AI Performance reports citations, cited pages, and sampled grounding queries. Grounding queries are retrieval phrases used by the system; they are not a transcript of customers’ questions. Citation counts are not visits, sales, or ranking positions. [Microsoft’s AI Performance explanation](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview/)

We completed Bing verification after publishing our changes on October 27. The sitemap was submitted and was processing. We had no usable Bing acquisition or citation baseline to report at that point. A new report that is still processing should be recorded as unavailable, not zero.

Use existing website analytics if you have them. Referrers can help identify some visits, but may be absent. Do not install a second tracker just to make a dashboard look complete, and do not label every direct visit as AI traffic.

## 8. Run one manageable 30-day experiment

Choose a small test you can maintain alongside your business.

**At the start:** Save your three discovery questions and their answers. Record what is indexed, what source data you have, and what is unknown. Choose one page to clarify or one access problem to fix. Record the actual publication date.

**When someone inquires:** Ask an optional question such as “How did you find us? Google, ChatGPT, another AI tool, a friend, or somewhere else?” Do not make someone complete a research questionnaire to request your service.

During an ordinary conversation, you can ask: “Do you remember what you searched for, and had you heard of us before?” Keep the response in their own words. It is self-reported evidence, not proof that one page change caused the sale.

**Once a week:** Spend about 15 minutes recording real inquiries, quotes, and payments. Use internal IDs instead of copying personal details into a shared tracker. Record payments when received; a $200 deposit is $200 collected, not the entire contract value. Track toolkit purchases separately from website-service customers.

Here is a fictional outcome row:

| Inquiry ID | Source stated | Knew us before | Qualified | Paid so far | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EXAMPLE-01 | ChatGPT | No | Yes | $200 deposit | Buyer said they asked for a local designer; exact answer not saved |

That supports “one buyer reported discovering us through ChatGPT and paid a deposit.” It does not support “AI optimization generated a full $900 sale” or prove which change mattered.

**After 30 days:** Repeat the same questions in the same modes. Compare equivalent report windows, allowing for reporting delays. Keep Google AI Mode, an AI Overview, and ChatGPT results separate. A new mention is a useful observation, not a controlled experiment proving causation.

Ask what changed at each stage: correct citations, relevant visits, qualified inquiries, paying customers, and money received. Also record time spent. If citations increase without buyer inquiries, investigate the page and audience before spending more. If someone pays, preserve the evidence trail without overstating certainty. If nothing improves, report that honestly and choose whether this work deserves more time.

### Where our experiment stands

Our technical changes are live as of October 27, 2026. Bing ownership is verified. The nine-answer discovery sample is our starting observation, not a post-publication result. Day-30 recommendations, qualified inquiries, paying customers, and attributable revenue have not yet been measured. We will update the case when those observations exist.
